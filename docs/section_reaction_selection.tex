\paragraph{Reaction selection}

We stratified rather than sampled. A random draw from the Transition1x test
split would have been dominated by closed-shell reactions with clean
single-determinant transition states, and the failure mode this work examines
would have been almost absent from it. We therefore ordered the candidate
reactions along an \emph{a priori} descriptor of static correlation and drew
deliberately from both ends of that order and from the interior.

The descriptor is $N_\mathrm{FOD}$, the fractional-occupation-number weighted
density,
%
\begin{equation}
  N_\mathrm{FOD} \;=\; \sum_i \bigl| n_i - n_i^{0} \bigr|
  \;=\; 2 \sum_{i \in \mathrm{virt}} n_i ,
\end{equation}
%
which counts the electron density that a Fermi-smeared calculation places in
orbitals a single determinant would leave empty. We computed it once per
reaction, at the reference transition state, with PBE/def2-SVP and Fermi
smearing at $T_\mathrm{el} = 5000$~K. Two properties make it the right
selection axis. It is cheap enough to run over the whole pool, and it is
computed before any of the quantities this paper measures, so the sample cannot
have been chosen by its outcome.

We inherited the pool rather than defining it. It holds the 279 reactions of
the Transition1x test split for which a converged reference NEB and its
transition state were already on disk from an earlier and unrelated stage of
this project; the remaining eight of the 287 test reactions were never
screened. We kept that boundary deliberately. It was fixed before the effect
reported here was known, so neither the pool nor the ranking can have been
shaped by an observation that did not yet exist.

Ranking the 279 by $N_\mathrm{FOD}$ spans a factor of nearly 400, from $0.003$
to $1.146$, and the strongly correlated tail is thin: the top 26 reactions are
$9\,\%$ of the pool. We drew three strata from this ranking.

Each stratum answers a different question. The \textbf{high} stratum is the stress
test: it concentrates the cases where a restricted reference is most likely to
be the wrong description, and it carries the effect we report. The \textbf{low}
stratum is the negative control. These reactions should show no symmetry
breaking at all, and they are where our null probes live -- where the
restricted and broken-symmetry surfaces coincide, the two calculations must
return the same energy and the same force, and any deviation is an error in the
pipeline rather than a result. The \textbf{spread} stratum fills the interior, so
the axis is covered continuously and not only at its ends.

The design worked as intended: 18 of the 45 reactions turn out to have a
broken-symmetry ground state at at least one transition state, and they fall
where the descriptor predicted -- 17 of the 26 high-$N_\mathrm{FOD}$
reactions, 1 of the 9 in the spread, and none of the 10 lowest. We stress that
this $40\,\%$ is a property of a deliberately enriched sample and not an
estimate of how often the problem occurs in Transition1x as a whole; the pool
was never screened for stability, only ranked.

Three boundaries of the selection are worth stating plainly. First, the eight
unscreened reactions are few, $2.8\,\%$ of the test split, but they are not
drawn uniformly: six of the eight share one molecular formula, which suggests
the earlier reference NEB converged less readily for that composition. We
report the boundary rather than repair it, since the pool predates this study.
Second,
$N_\mathrm{FOD}$ sits at PBE/def2-SVP with smearing, a level used nowhere else
in this work; it enters the paper only as the axis along which the sample was
drawn and contributes no measured quantity to any result. Third, the sample is
chemically narrow. All 45 systems are neutral singlets built from C, H, N and O
with 40 to 52 electrons, distributed over five molecular formulas. The
mechanism we describe is electronic and should not depend on that composition,
but we have not tested it outside these bounds.
