% 2.1 Computational Details -- DFT reference
\subsection{Computational Details}
\label{sec:dft}
\paragraph{DFT reference.}
Reference energies and forces are computed at the OMol25 level of theory, $\omega$B97M-V/def2-TZVPD\cite{mardirossian2016wb97mv,weigend2005def2}, with ORCA 6.0.1\cite{orca6} (RI-J, COSX, DEFGRID3, tight SCF convergence, as in OMol25\cite{omol25}). Each structure is computed in the unrestricted Kohn--Sham (UKS) formalism from an unperturbed initial guess, which for a singlet converges to the closed-shell solution. A wavefunction stability analysis\cite{seeger1977,bauernschmitt1996} then tests this solution. If it is unstable, the SCF is reoptimized on the lower-energy, broken-symmetry solution. Energy and forces are taken from the final solution.

OMol25 instead broke the spin symmetry of the initial guess by rotating the $\beta$ HOMO and LUMO by 20$^\circ$\cite{omol25}. If that SCF returns to the closed-shell solution, it remains open whether a lower solution exists. The stability analysis answers this question. Where both protocols reach the same solution, our energies and forces agree with the OMol25 labels to within 0.1\,meV in energy and 0.013\,eV/\AA{} in the largest force component (SI, Section~S1). For a small share of structures, the stability analysis finds a lower solution than the OMol25 label (Section~\ref{sec:labelcheck}).

\paragraph{Multireference character.}
As a measure of multireference character, we use the fractional occupation number weighted electron density, $N_\mathrm{FOD}$\cite{grimme2015fod}. It counts the electrons in fractionally occupied orbitals of a finite-temperature DFT calculation. Values near zero mean that a single determinant describes the structure well; values around 1 and above indicate strong static correlation. We compute $N_\mathrm{FOD}$ with PBE/def2-SVP at $T_\mathrm{el} = 5000$\,K in PySCF 2.12.1\cite{pyscf}.

% 2.1 Computational Details -- spin class
\paragraph{Spin class.}
A structure is \emph{broken-symmetry} if its final UKS solution has $\langle S^2\rangle > 0.05$, and \emph{closed-shell} otherwise.

% Marks et al. used UMA-S 1.1, UMA-M 1.1, eSEN-S, MACE-OMol25 (marks.txt l. 285-297).

\paragraph{Models.}
We test four machine-learned interatomic potentials trained on OMol25: eSEN-sm-conserving\cite{fu2025esen}, UMA-S 1.2 and UMA-M 1.1\cite{wood2025uma}, and MACE-OMol-0 (extra large)\cite{batatia2025maceomol}. All four are trained to reproduce $\omega$B97M-V/def2-TZVPD energies and forces. They are the models benchmarked by Marks et al.\cite{marks2026fsm}, except that we use the newer UMA-S 1.2. eSEN and MACE-OMol are trained on OMol25 alone and differ in architecture. The two UMA models are also trained on datasets of materials, surfaces and molecular crystals, and differ in size. eSEN and the UMA models run in single precision, the fairchem default, and MACE-OMol in double precision.

Every structure is given to the models as a neutral singlet (charge 0, spin multiplicity 1). The models have no input that distinguishes a closed-shell from a broken-symmetry solution; what they predict for a broken-symmetry reaction follows from the labels they were trained on.

\subsection{Reactions}
\label{sec:reactions}

We draw reactions from Transition1x,\cite{schreiner2022transition1x} a dataset of 10,073 gas-phase reactions of molecules containing C, H, N and O. Transition1x was generated with nudged elastic band calculations at the $\omega$B97X/6-31G(d) level, finished with a climbing image, and stores the structures of all band iterations, about 9.6 million in total. For each reaction, it also provides the reactant, the product and the converged transition state (the climbing image), which we call the Transition1x transition state. OMol25\cite{levine2025omol25} recomputed the Transition1x structures at $\omega$B97M-V/def2-TZVPD and includes them in its training data.

Most Transition1x transition states have low multireference character (Figure~\ref{fgr:landscape}): 16\,\% have $N_\mathrm{FOD} \geq 0.5$ and 2\,\% have $N_\mathrm{FOD} \geq 1$, and broken symmetry becomes common only at high $N_\mathrm{FOD}$. A random sample would therefore contain few of the reactions we are interested in.

\subsection{Evaluation at the Transition1x Transition States}
\label{sec:evalt1x}
We first evaluate at the Transition1x transition states, which are part of the OMol25 training data. To check the reference, we recompute all 10,009 of them with the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:dft} and compare energy, forces and spin class with the OMol25 labels. To measure how well the models fit their training data, we evaluate each model at the same transition states and compare with the OMol25 labels. The \emph{force error} is the mean absolute difference between model and reference force components,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MAE}_F = \frac{1}{3N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{\alpha=x,y,z} \left| F^\mathrm{model}_{i\alpha} - F^\mathrm{ref}_{i\alpha} \right|,
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the number of atoms. $N_\mathrm{FOD}$ is computed at the same structures.

% 2.4 Transition-state search. Settings: pipeline/fsm_search.py
\subsection{Transition-State Search}
\label{sec:search}
In practice, a model is used to locate a transition state, and the result depends on how accurate the model is at the structure it finds. We therefore let each model search for the transition state itself, with the workflow that Marks et al.\cite{marks2026fsm} found reliable for these models: the freezing string method\cite{behn2011fsm} builds a path between reactant and product and gives a guess for the transition state, which Sella\cite{hermes2022sella} then refines to a first-order saddle point on the model surface. Reactant and product are the Transition1x structures, first relaxed on each model (BFGS, 0.05\,eV/\AA); the search does not use the Transition1x path or transition state. Settings are given in SI Section~S3.

We call the converged structure the located transition state. Of the 2,040 searches (510 reactions, four models), 1,672 located a transition state. Almost all failures occurred while the freezing string was built and depended on the reaction, not on the model (SI, Section~S4). A located transition state need not connect the intended reactant and product; it can, for example, lead to an intermediate. We check this by displacing it along its imaginary mode and relaxing both sides on the model (SI, Section~S4). The check is reported but not used as a filter: every located transition state enters the error analysis in Section~\ref{sec:errors} and Section~\ref{sec:results}, because the model can be compared with DFT at any structure it identifies as a transition state.

\subsection{Evaluation at the Located Transition States}
\label{sec:errors}
At every located transition state we compute the DFT reference, $N_\mathrm{FOD}$ and the spin class as described in Section~\ref{sec:dft}, so that the error and the multireference character are measured at the same structure.

We use two error measures: the force error of Section~\ref{sec:evalt1x}, now against our DFT reference, and the \emph{barrier error}, the difference between the model and the DFT barrier,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\Delta E^\ddagger = \Delta E^\ddagger_\mathrm{model} - \Delta E^\ddagger_\mathrm{DFT}, \qquad \Delta E^\ddagger = E(\mathrm{TS}) - E(\mathrm{R}),
\end{equation}
where both barriers are evaluated at the same two structures, the located transition state and the reactant the model relaxed for its search, so that the barrier error contains no difference in geometry. We compare barrier errors with chemical accuracy, 1\,kcal/mol (43\,meV).

In both evaluations, we summarise errors by their median and give 95\,\% confidence intervals for ratios of medians from 5{,}000 bootstrap resamples.
